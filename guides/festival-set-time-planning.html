    How to Plan a Festival Day From the Set Times

    Facts checked against the sources below on 2026-10-18.

    Quick answer: Get the official app, wait for the real set times, then rank every set must-see, maybe, or skip. Leave a buffer for walking, put water and food into the plan like they are sets, and pick a backup for every clash. Set times can change, so recheck the day before and the morning of.

    You paid for the ticket. Now you have a lineup, a map, and a clash that hurts. Here is how we plan a festival day, step by step.

    Step 1: Get the official app and know where set times show up

    Lineup and set times are two different things. The lineup tells you who is playing. Set times tell you who plays when, on which stage.

    Festivals publish set times on their own channels. EDC Las Vegas says set times get posted before the festival through its app, social media, and email, and that the lineup is subject to change [1]. Electric Forest has a set-times page that lets you build your own schedule in its app [2]. Lollapalooza points fans to a mobile app and a schedule page on its site [3].

    Timing is not the same everywhere. Some festivals put times out well ahead and others do it close to the weekend. We will not guess a date for any 2027 event. Check the festival's own site and app, and turn notifications on.

    Download the app at home on good wifi. Do not try it for the first time at the gate with a huge crowd on the same cell tower.

    Step 2: Rank every set: must-see, maybe, skip

    Open the schedule and go through it once, fast, with no planning. Mark each set one of three ways.

    • Must-see: you would be upset to miss it. Keep this list short. Five to eight sets a day is plenty for most people.
    • Maybe: you like them, and you would go if nothing better is on.
    • Skip: nothing wrong with them. You just need the break.

    Most apps let you star sets. Star the must-sees first. Then share the list with your group so everyone sees the same picture.

    Step 3: Read the overlaps

    Now lay your must-sees on a timeline. Clashes will jump out.

    Three things to check on every clash:

    1. Is it a true overlap or a partial one? Many clashes are only 15 or 20 minutes. You can catch the first half of one set and the second half of the other.
    2. Which one is rarer? If one artist tours your city every year and the other has not been near you in ages, the rare one wins.
    3. Which one needs the best spot? A big headliner in a packed crowd is fine from the back. A smaller, heavier set might be one you want near the front. Plan to arrive early for that one.

    Decide the clash now. Do not make the call at the exact moment two sets start.

    Step 4: Add walking time between stages

    Stages are not next to each other, and crowds slow you down. We do not know your pace or the festival's layout, so we will not put a number on it. Look at the festival map in the app and make a guess. Then add more than you think you need.

    A few tips from us:

    • Leave a set 10 to 15 minutes before it ends if the next one is far away.
    • Walking against a crowd leaving a main stage is slower than walking on an empty field. Leave earlier, or wait it out.
    • Pick a meeting spot per stage. Cell service can be bad in big crowds, so do not count on texting.

    Step 5: Put water, food, and rest in the plan

    Treat these like sets. If they are not on the schedule, they will not happen.

    The CDC says to drink plenty of non-alcoholic fluids, rest often, and stay in the shade when you can [4]. It also says to plan outdoor activities during cooler parts of the day [4]. That is useful for festival days. If it is hot, the afternoon is a good time for the sets you do not care about, and a break.

    Know the warning signs. The CDC lists intense thirst, heavy sweating, headache, dizziness or confusion, and nausea as signs of heat exhaustion. If you feel that, get out of direct sun right away, cool down (fan, air conditioning, or cool water), and drink cool non-alcoholic beverages such as water or sports drinks with electrolytes [4]. Heat stroke is a medical emergency, so get medical help right away [4].

    Practical version:

    • Block a food break during a "skip" slot. Do not eat during the headliner you came for.
    • Find the water stations on the map when you arrive. EDC says it provides free drinking water in its campground [1]. Check your festival's own page for what is available on the grounds.
    • Plan a 20-minute sit-down once, mid-afternoon.
    • Charge your phone. Bring a spare battery if the rules allow it.

    Step 6: Check the re-entry rule

    This changes your plan more than you think. EDC says ins-and-outs are not allowed [1]. Ultra says there is no re-entry once you are inside [5]. So if you leave to eat, nap, or grab something from the car, you may not get back in. Read your festival's FAQ before you build a plan that depends on leaving.

    Step 7: Build a backup plan

    Things change. A set gets moved. A stage runs late. A storm delays things. Check the festival's app and official channels for changes [1]. EDC says the lineup is subject to change [1].

    So for every must-see, have a backup:

    • If a set moves, check which maybe-set now fits.
    • If you lose your group, go to the meeting spot, not around the whole field.
    • If you are wiped, drop one maybe and keep the must-sees. That is a good trade.
    • If your phone dies, know where the paper guide is. EDC says set times also appear on the festival guide you get at entry [1].

    Step 8: Recheck the plan the day before and the morning of

    Open the app again the night before. Open it again at breakfast. Updates happen. Then put your phone on low-power mode, and go.

    FAQ

    When do festivals release set times?

    It depends on the festival. EDC says before the festival, through its app, social media, and email [1]. We will not claim dates for 2027. Check the official site and app.

    Can set times change after they are posted?

    Yes. EDC says the lineup is subject to change [1].

    Should I plan every minute?

    No. Plan the must-sees, the clashes, and the breaks. Leave the rest open.
